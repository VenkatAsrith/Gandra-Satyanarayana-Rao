Early Life & Heritage

Agrarian Roots in Buddharam & Decades of Grassroots Dedication

Gandra Satyanarayana Rao, affectionately addressed across northern Telangana as "Sathenna" (సత్తెన్న), was born in 1965 in the agrarian village of Buddharam (also written as Bhudharam) in Ghanapuram Mandal, Jayashankar Bhupalpally district, Telangana. Born to Late Sri Gandra Samma Rao, an upright agrarian elder, Sathenna's character was forged in the agricultural heartland among farmers, rural artisans, and coal field laborers.

Throughout his four-decade public career, agriculture has remained his enduring vocation. His hands-on understanding of village water tanks, canal ayacuts, and agricultural power supply drove him into public life at the grassroots level, rising from Village Sarpanch to ZPTC member, before earning a historic election to the Telangana Legislative Assembly in 2023 with a resounding 52,699-vote victory margin.

Grassroots Philosophy

Accessible Representation & The Weekly "Praja Darbar"

Known throughout the constituency as a leader whose doors never close to the common citizen, Sathenna has instituted the weekly Praja Darbar at his Bhupalpalle MLA Camp Office. Every week, farmers, widows seeking pensions, youth requesting education aid, and workers facing administrative delays present their grievances directly to him.

Rather than delegating constituent grievances to intermediaries, Sathenna summons local mandal revenue officers (MROs), electricity department engineers, and municipal officers to resolve public issues on the spot. His greeting of folded hands has become an emblem of dignity, humility, and accessibility in local governance.

State Governance & Partnership

State Collaboration with Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy

Accompanied by his spouse Smt. Gandra Padma, Sathenna maintains strategic alignment with Telangana Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy and the state cabinet. This direct coordination has unlocked major budgetary sanctions for Bhupalpalle, including the ₹31 Crore Ghanapur canal modernization, ₹12.15 Crore Kotanchu temple infrastructure, and new 33/11 KV power substations.

Their shared vision focuses on transforming Jayashankar Bhupalpally from an economically backward border district into an agrarian powerhouse with world-class mining labor protections and modern rural road networks.

Legislative & Mass Action

A Resolute Voice on the Stage & in the Legislative Assembly

Whether addressing massive public congregations alongside the TPCC leadership or articulating pressing rural issues on the floor of the Telangana Legislative Assembly, Sathenna is recognized for his fearless, evidence-based advocacy.

In September 2026, he led assembly debates demanding full safety equipment, air-conditioned mining rest zones, and prompt compassionate job appointments for Singareni Collieries workers, alongside urgent cold storage infrastructure for vegetable and oil palm farmers.

2014 & 18 Assembly Contests

Formidable Independent Grassroots Base

Contested the Bhupalpalle Assembly constituency in 2014 (polling over 50,000 votes) and in 2018 on an All India Forward Bloc (AIFB) ticket, where he secured an astonishing 53,982 votes as an anti-establishment independent force, demonstrating profound mass appeal across rural voters.

Source: Election Commission of India Official Results
2021 Congress Induction

Induction into Indian National Congress

Formally inducted into the Indian National Congress on September 30, 2021, under the leadership of TPCC President A. Revanth Reddy. Galvanized grassroots booth-level committees, youth wings, and farmer groups across all 6 mandals.

Source: The New Indian Express & The Hans India
2023 Elected MLA

Dec 3, 2023: Decisive Assembly Landslide (52,699 Margin)

Elected to the 3rd Telangana Legislative Assembly representing Bhupalpalle constituency (No. 108) with a landslide 123,116 votes (54.55% of the total vote share), defeating sitting BRS incumbent and former government Chief Whip Gandra Venkata Ramana Reddy by an unprecedented margin of 52,699 votes. Currently serving his five-year term (2023–2028).

Source: Election Commission of India Gazette / PRS Legislative Research
